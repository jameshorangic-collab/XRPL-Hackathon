# Competitive landscape and positioning

BACKSTOP does not claim to invent subordination or structured credit. Its tested feature is competitive allocation of separately quoted senior and junior funding under a small, explainable constraint model.

| Established approach                           | Relevant precedent                                                                                     | BACKSTOP prototype scope                                                                                                                         |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Warehouse lending and senior credit facilities | Originators finance loan assets with lender eligibility, advance, and protection requirements.         | Competes submitted senior quotes against external junior funding in a one-period facility. No revolving borrowing base or collateral monitoring. |
| Loan syndication                               | Multiple investors can finance an exposure.                                                            | Multiple external junior investors are supported; the senior side selects one primary lender.                                                    |
| Credit enhancement and securitization          | Priority classes and retained subordinated exposure can redistribute portfolio losses.                 | Funded stack and explicit cash priorities, without legal asset transfer, SPV, rating, or securities issuance.                                    |
| Goldfinch                                      | Its V1 documentation describes Backers funding junior first-loss positions and a separate senior pool. | Independently quoted senior/junior offers compete on total monetary cost rather than an automatic leverage schedule.                             |
| Centrifuge                                     | V2 documentation describes tranche priorities and an epoch solver for investment/redemption orders.    | A deliberately smaller initial financing problem; it does not claim novelty for tranche solvers generally.                                       |
| Maple                                          | Protocol roles include pool delegates administering and funding loans.                                 | A capital-formation demonstration rather than a full delegated credit origination system.                                                        |

The [OCC securitization overview](https://occ.treas.gov/topics/supervision-and-examination/capital-markets/financial-markets/securitization/index-securitization.html) and [handbook](https://occ.treas.gov/publications-and-resources/publications/comptrollers-handbook/files/asset-securitization/pub-ch-asset-securitization.pdf) explain established asset financing and credit enhancement. These references establish precedent; they do not validate BACKSTOP's economics or regulatory status.

The comparisons with on-chain protocols use their own documentation: [Goldfinch capital providers](https://docs.goldfinch.finance/goldfinch/goldfinch-v1/borrower-mechanics/goldfinchs-capital-providers), [Centrifuge V2](https://docs.centrifuge.io/build/cent-chain/), and [Maple protocol actors](https://docs.maple.finance/technical-resources/protocol-overview/protocol-actors). V1/V2 references are historical design precedents, not assertions that every current product offers identical features.

The commercial hypothesis is that standardized smaller portfolios and competitive quote matching could reduce coordination costs. Actual capital cost reductions must be measured after fees, retained-equity economics, legal work, and servicing costs. Customer demand, junior-investor supply, and lower execution cost remain unverified.
